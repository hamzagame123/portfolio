THE BRIEF

Design a mobile app centered around farmers markets that builds community and makes local food accessible

Create an app that helps users discover and track farmers markets in their city, see which friends are attending, coordinate meetups, and connect with local farmers and vendors, turning market day into a community event.

Target Users

Farmers market enthusiasts, eco-conscious consumers, community-minded locals

Core Challenge

Turn farmers market visits into social experiences while helping users track multiple markets

Impact Goal

Increase farmers market attendance, build community connections, support local vendors

THE PROBLEM

A broken food system hurts everyone

The current agricultural supply chain creates a lose-lose situation: farmers struggle to find markets while consumers pay inflated prices for produce that's traveled thousands of miles.

"It is difficult sourcing a market for my product most of the time. I highly rely on middlemen who exploit us and buy at really low prices."

Local Farmer, Interview

"I'm forced to rely on brokers to get products. They sell expensively and that affects my business as I'm forced to sell at higher prices."

Small Business Owner, Interview

RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

How I Uncovered User Needs

A structured 4-phase approach to understanding the problem space

Phase 1

Secondary Research

Methods Used

  • Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada data analysis
  • Academic papers on local food systems
  • Competitive landscape mapping
  • Industry reports on food miles

Key Finding

There are over 800 farmers markets across Canada (Statistics Canada, 2023), but 73% of consumers report difficulty locating reliable local food sources. The average produce travels 2,500 km—creating a clear opportunity.

Phase 2

User Interviews

Participants

  • 3 regular farmers market shoppers
  • Varied frequency: weekly to monthly
  • Mix of solo and social market-goers
  • 30-45 minute semi-structured interviews

Key Finding

"I constantly forget it's on" was mentioned by all 3 participants. Users also wanted to know who's attending before committing—social proof matters.

Phase 3

Affinity Mapping

Process

  • 25 insights from 3 interviews
  • Clustered into 4 theme groups
  • Prioritized by frequency & impact
  • Identified design opportunities

Key Finding

Four themes emerged: scheduling/remembering (32%), social coordination (28%), vendor discovery (24%), and impact/values (16%). These became the core feature pillars.

Phase 4

Usability Testing

Approach

  • 5 participants (mixed experience)
  • Task-based evaluation
  • Think-aloud protocol
  • SUS questionnaire post-test

Key Finding

92% task completion rate. "I'm Going" button was immediately understood. Users loved seeing friends—but wanted easier invite flow (iterated).

DESIGN CHALLENGES

UX Obstacles & Solutions

Key design challenges and how I addressed them

01

Multiple Markets, One Interface

Challenge

Cities have dozens of farmers markets with different schedules, locations, and vendors, which can be overwhelming to track

Design Solution

Calendar-style date badges, location-based sorting, favorite markets list, and clear "This Week" grouping to surface what's relevant now

02

Social Without Being Creepy

Challenge

Users want to know who's attending, but location-based social features can feel invasive

Design Solution

Opt-in "I'm Going" model: you only appear if you actively mark attendance. Friends-only visibility options. No real-time location tracking

03

Vendor Continuity Across Markets

Challenge

Users find a great vendor at one market but don't know where else they sell. Vendors often appear at multiple markets

Design Solution

Unified vendor profiles showing all markets they attend, follow functionality with notifications, and "Where are they this week?" discovery

04

Encouraging Commitment

Challenge

Users browse markets but don't commit to going, showing high intent but low follow-through

Design Solution

Social proof ("Sarah & 2 friends going"), prominent "I'm Going" CTA, reminder notifications, and FOMO-inducing friend activity feeds

Trust Is the Product

In a marketplace connecting strangers, every design decision must answer: "Does this make users feel safe?" Verified badges, ratings, and messaging aren't just features; they're the foundation.

🎯

Quantify the Abstract

"Sustainable" means nothing until it's "2.3kg CO₂ saved." Turning eco-impact into numbers makes it real, shareable, and motivating. Gamification (Earth Guardian levels) adds emotional reward.

🎨

Design Signals Quality

Users judge reliability by aesthetics before functionality. The polished green theme and Fraunces typography signal "premium" and "trustworthy," which is critical for food purchases from strangers.

🔄

Two Users, One Vision

Designing for both farmers and buyers required understanding their opposing pain points, then finding features that solve both: direct messaging helps buyers trust AND helps farmers explain.

REFLECTION

Personal Takeaways

This project taught me that sustainable design isn't just about environmental impact. It's about creating systems that sustain trust, relationships, and communities. The Locavore movement isn't just about food miles; it's about reconnecting people with their food sources and each other.

The most challenging aspect was designing for two fundamentally different user types with opposing needs. Farmers need efficient tools to list products and manage orders; buyers need confidence-building features and easy discovery. Finding the overlap (direct messaging, verified profiles, transparent pricing) was the key insight.

If I were to start over, I would conduct more in-depth farmer interviews earlier in the process. While buyer needs are well-documented in market research, farmer pain points around technology adoption and digital literacy would benefit from more ethnographic research.
